================================================================================
RAG PIPELINE EVALUATION: RESEARCH RESULTS SUMMARY
================================================================================
Dataset: 13,095 evaluation records
LLMs: 5 (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o Mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Llama 3.1 70B, Mistral Large)
Chunking Strategies: 9 (fixed_128/256/512, semantic_128/256/512, lc_semantic_128/256/512)
Domains: 3 (general, medical, finance)
Query Types: 4 (single-hop, multi-hop, comparative, unanswerable)
Evaluation: LLM-as-Judge (GPT-4o-mini) using 1-5 Likert scale normalized to [0,1]
  Based on: Zheng et al. (2023), Liu et al. (2023) G-Eval, Saad-Falcon et al. (2023) ARES


================================================================================
1. OVERALL PERFORMANCE
================================================================================

Metric                  Mean     SD       Median
-----------------------------------------------
LLM Judge Score         0.8254   0.3226   1.0000
Answer Relevancy        0.9547   0.1223   1.0000
Faithfulness            0.5944   0.1948   0.6000
Context Precision       0.5586   0.4656   0.8333
Context Recall          0.5529   0.4533   0.6842

Key finding: RAG systems achieved high answer correctness (82.5%) and relevancy
(95.5%), but faithfulness (59.4%) — the degree to which answers are grounded in
retrieved context — remains a significant limitation across all configurations.


================================================================================
2. LLM PERFORMANCE COMPARISON
================================================================================

LLM                  Judge Score   Relevancy   Faithfulness
-----------------------------------------------------------
Mistral Large        0.8769        0.9876      0.5107
GPT-4o Mini          0.8303        0.9767      0.6416
Claude 3.5 Sonnet    0.8300        0.9569      0.5772
Llama 3.1 70B        0.8152        0.9529      0.6413
Gemini 2.5 Pro       0.7747        0.8996      0.6014

Key findings:
- Mistral Large achieved the highest correctness score (0.877) but the lowest
  faithfulness (0.511), suggesting it draws heavily on parametric knowledge
  rather than retrieved context.
- GPT-4o Mini and Llama 3.1 70B achieved the best faithfulness scores (~0.64),
  indicating stronger grounding in retrieved context.
- Gemini 2.5 Pro scored lowest on correctness (0.775) and relevancy (0.900),
  potentially due to more conservative or hedged responses.
- There is an inverse relationship between correctness and faithfulness for
  some models, suggesting a trade-off between leveraging parametric knowledge
  and staying grounded in context.

3. CHUNKING STRATEGY ANALYSIS
================================================================================

Strategy            Judge Score   Faithfulness   Ctx Precision   Ctx Recall
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
fixed_128           0.8048        0.5767         0.5391          0.5355
fixed_256           0.8340        0.6130         0.5694          0.5702
fixed_512           0.8289        0.6388         0.5928          0.5956
semantic_128        0.8196        0.5612         0.5542          0.5330
semantic_256        0.8313        0.6135         0.5631          0.5658
semantic_512        0.8330        0.6393         0.5848          0.5895
lc_semantic_128     0.8093        0.5597         0.5365          0.5194
lc_semantic_256     0.8282        0.5728         0.5440          0.5373
lc_semantic_512     0.8397        0.5751         0.5439          0.5302

Key findings:
- Larger chunk sizes (512) consistently outperformed smaller sizes (128) across
  all metrics, with the most pronounced improvements in faithfulness (+7 points)
  and context recall (+4 points).
- The difference between fixed and semantic chunking methods was NOT statistically
  significant for any metric (all p > 0.05, all Cohen's d < 0.03).
- LangChain semantic chunking showed marginally higher correctness at 512 tokens
  (0.8397) but lower context recall than fixed/semantic at the same size.

================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------
Judge Score         0.8226   0.8279     -0.776   0.438     -0.017
Answer Relevancy    0.9558   0.9548      0.366   0.714      0.008

Context Precision   0.5671   0.5673     -0.023   0.982     -0.001
Context Recall      0.5671   0.5628      0.445   0.657      0.010

Conclusion: No statistically significant differences were found between fixed
and semantic chunking across any evaluation metric. Effect sizes are negligible
(all |d| < 0.03). This suggests that for the datasets and configurations tested,
the choice between fixed and semantic chunking does not materially impact RAG
performance. Chunk SIZE matters more than chunk METHOD.


================================================================================
5. CHUNK SIZE ANALYSIS
================================================================================

Chunk Size   Judge Score   Faithfulness   Ctx Precision   Ctx Recall
--------------------------------------------------------------------
128          0.8112        0.5659         0.5433          0.5293
256          0.8312        0.5997         0.5588          0.5578
512          0.8338        0.6177         0.5738          0.5718

Key finding: Increasing chunk size from 128 to 512 tokens improved all metrics.
The most substantial gains were in faithfulness (+5.2 points) and context recall
(+4.3 points), indicating that larger chunks provide more complete context for
answer generation. Diminishing returns appear between 256 and 512.


================================================================================
6. DOMAIN ANALYSIS
================================================================================

Domain       N       Judge Score   Faithfulness   Ctx Precision   Ctx Recall
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
General      8,595   0.8645        0.6296         0.5776          0.6550
Medical      2,250   0.8727        0.5543         0.9958          0.6159
Finance      2,250   0.6288        0.5005         0.0489          0.1000

Key findings:
- Medical domain achieved excellent context precision (0.996), suggesting the
  retrieval system effectively surfaces relevant medical passages, though
  faithfulness (0.554) lags behind general domain.
- Finance domain performed significantly worse across all metrics, particularly
  context precision (0.049) and context recall (0.100). This indicates severe
  retrieval failures — the vector store is not retrieving relevant financial
  passages, leading to poor answer quality (0.629).
- The finance retrieval failure likely stems from the nature of financial data
  (tables, numerical data) being poorly represented in embedding space compared
  to narrative text.

7. QUERY TYPE ANALYSIS
================================================================================

Query Type       N       Judge Score   Faithfulness   Ctx Precision   Ctx Recall
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Comparative      1,305   0.8389        0.5517         0.1543          0.1954
Multi-hop        2,835   0.8213        0.6142         0.6668          0.8058
Single-hop       7,650   0.8296        0.5841         0.6828          0.6145

Key findings:
- Multi-hop queries achieved the highest context recall (0.806), suggesting the
  retrieval system successfully surfaces multiple relevant passages.
- Comparative queries had very low context precision (0.154) and recall (0.195),
  indicating difficulty retrieving passages that support comparison tasks.
- Unanswerable queries showed 0.0 context precision and recall (expected, as no
  relevant context exists), yet achieved a correctness score of 0.796, suggesting
  most LLMs appropriately recognize and decline unanswerable questions.
- Correctness scores were relatively stable across query types (0.80-0.84),
  indicating LLMs can compensate for retrieval gaps with parametric knowledge.

9. EVALUATION METHODOLOGY NOTE
================================================================================

LLM Judge Score Distribution (GPT-4o-mini, 1-5 Likert scale):
  Score 5 (Fully Correct):     9,189 (70.2%)
  Score 4 (Mostly Correct):    1,822 (13.9%)
  Score 3 (Partially Correct):    57  (0.4%)
  Score 2 (Mostly Incorrect):    899  (6.9%)
  Score 1 (Incorrect):         1,128  (8.6%)

The previous token-level F1 metric (bag-of-words overlap) produced a mean of
0.134, severely underestimating actual performance due to the mismatch between
short ground truth answers and verbose LLM responses. The correlation between
token F1 and LLM judge scores is weak (Pearson r = 0.292), confirming that
token F1 is an inappropriate metric for evaluating generative QA systems.

The LLM-as-Judge approach follows established methodology from Zheng et al.
(2023) and Liu et al. (2023), using a structured rubric with a 1-5 Likert
scale. A known limitation is potential judge bias — GPT-4o-mini may score
GPT-generated responses more favorably than those from other models. This
should be noted in the paper's limitations section.


================================================================================
10. KEY CONCLUSIONS FOR RESEARCH PAPER
================================================================================

1. RAG systems achieve high answer correctness (82.5%) across diverse domains
   and query types, but faithfulness — grounding answers in retrieved context —
   remains the primary bottleneck at 59.4%.

2. Chunk SIZE matters significantly more than chunk METHOD. Larger chunks (512
   tokens) consistently outperform smaller chunks (128 tokens) across all
   metrics. Fixed vs. semantic chunking shows no statistically significant
   difference (all p > 0.05).

3. Domain suitability is critical. Finance domain shows near-total retrieval
   failure (context precision: 4.9%), suggesting embedding-based retrieval is
   poorly suited for numerical/tabular financial data without domain-specific
   adaptation.

4. LLMs exhibit a correctness-faithfulness trade-off. Mistral Large achieves
   the highest correctness but lowest faithfulness, implying heavy reliance on
   parametric knowledge. GPT-4o Mini and Llama 3.1 70B offer the best balance.

5. The optimal configuration is GPT-4o Mini with 512-token fixed or semantic
   chunks (overall score: 0.737). This configuration maximizes both answer
   quality and faithfulness to retrieved context.

6. Token-level F1 is an inappropriate metric for generative RAG evaluation
   (r = 0.292 with human-aligned LLM judge scores). LLM-as-Judge evaluation
   should be the standard for future RAG research.
